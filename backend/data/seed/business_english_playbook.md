# Business English Field Notes

## Clear requests

A useful request normally contains context, the requested action, and a realistic deadline. Prefer “Could you send the revised forecast by 3 p.m. Thursday?” over “Please send it soon.” Use “Could you” for a professional request and “Would you be able to” when the request is unusually demanding.

## Meetings and alignment

“Align” means reaching a shared understanding, not necessarily agreeing on every detail. Summarize decisions with an owner and deadline: “We agreed that Maya will validate the figures by Friday.” Replace vague endings such as “Let’s discuss later” with a named next step.

## Constructive disagreement

Lead with the shared goal, identify the specific concern, and offer an alternative. Example: “I agree that speed matters. My concern is the support risk. Could we pilot this with ten customers first?” Avoid “You are wrong” and empty softeners that hide the point.

## Email structure

For routine business email, use: informative subject, purpose in the first sentence, necessary context, explicit action, deadline, and a short closing. Readers should not have to infer what response is expected.

## Negotiation

Separate positions from interests. Instead of immediately accepting or rejecting a price, clarify scope, timing, volume, and risk. Conditional language such as “If we commit to twelve months, could you offer…” makes a trade explicit.

## Presentations

Signpost the argument: “The key point is…”, “There are two reasons…”, and “What this means for us is…”. Numbers require a baseline and period. Replace “sales increased a lot” with “monthly recurring revenue rose 18% quarter over quarter.”
